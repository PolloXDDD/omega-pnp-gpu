\section{What Remains for a P=NP Theorem}
\label{sec:pnp-roadmap}

The present construction separates the remaining burden into explicit
mathematical statements.

The exact encoding already provides a polynomial-size sparse objective
$E_F$ such that
\begin{equation}
\min E_F=0
\iff
F\in\SAT.
\end{equation}
The remaining task is not the SAT encoding; it is exact global optimization.

A sufficient deterministic theorem would be the following.

\begin{theorem}[Target theorem]
There exists a deterministic polynomial-time transition rule and a polynomial
$p$ such that, for every 3-CNF formula $F$, every run of the rule visits a
global minimum of $E_F$ within at most
\begin{equation}
p(|F|)
\end{equation}
updates.
\end{theorem}

If this theorem holds, the algorithm records the smallest energy encountered.
After $p(|F|)$ steps it returns SAT if the recorded minimum is zero and UNSAT
otherwise.  Hence 3-SAT would lie in P and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{P=NP.}
\end{equation}

The current stochastic focused dynamics does not yet establish this theorem.
A polynomial block-contraction bound would imply polynomial expected hitting
time for satisfiable instances and would support a randomized SAT algorithm,
but randomized expected convergence alone is not a proof that SAT belongs to
deterministic P.

There are therefore three remaining proof obligations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Uniform global-minimum visitation.}
      Prove a polynomial bound that applies to every 3-CNF formula, not only
      planted or finite adversarial samples.

\item \textbf{UNSAT decision.}
      Positive-energy states cannot be recognized as globally optimal from
      local information alone.  A deterministic visitation bound resolves this
      by allowing the best energy seen during the complete certified run to be
      used as the decision value.

\item \textbf{Polynomial derandomization or deterministic progress.}
      Replace stochastic exploration by a deterministic rule, or construct a
      polynomially computable potential whose value decreases by an inverse
      polynomial amount under a deterministically selectable update.
\end{enumerate}

The exact hitting-time function $H_F$ proves that a perfect progress potential
exists for each finite satisfiable instance.  The current central question is
whether its useful information can be represented and updated using only
polynomial resources.
