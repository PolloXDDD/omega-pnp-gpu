\section{First-Passage Tests on UNSAT Instances}
\label{sec:unsat-first-passage}

For an unsatisfiable formula the focused dynamics never encounters zero
energy.  Nevertheless, a certified global-optimization algorithm need only
visit a global minimum; it may record the best energy encountered and continue.

For diagnostic purposes, the set of exact global minima was therefore made
absorbing only in the first-passage analysis.  Twenty random strict UNSAT
instances were generated at each tested size with $m=6n$, and the worst sample
by expected first-passage time is reported below.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Worst random UNSAT first-passage sample at each tested size.}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & $m$ & $E_{\min}$ & $\max H_F$ & $\varepsilon_{n^2}$ &
$n^2/\varepsilon_{n^2}$ \\
\midrule
8 & 48 & 1 & 69.175 & 0.5917 & 108.2 \\
10 & 60 & 1 & 128.332 & 0.5278 & 189.5 \\
12 & 72 & 2 & 133.198 & 0.6642 & 216.8 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

These experiments show that the same first-passage framework can study positive
global minima.  They do not supply a stopping certificate to the actual
algorithm: that requires the uniform deterministic visitation theorem stated
in Section~\ref{sec:pnp-roadmap}.
